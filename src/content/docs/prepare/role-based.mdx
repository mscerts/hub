## Who Are These Exams For?

Role-based exams cover three certification levels:

- **Associate**: Best for working professionals who want to validate product skills, with a focus on deeper technical knowledge and practical implementation.
- **Expert**: Best for experienced cloud professionals and architects, with a focus on technical and architectural depth across services, SKUs, and design decisions.
- **Specialty**: Best for professionals specializing in a specific Microsoft product area, with a focus on deep product-specific knowledge and how that product fits into the cloud ecosystem.

## Planning Your Preparation

Preparation time varies with your experience, the breadth of the study guide, access to a suitable environment, and the number of hours you can study each week. Estimate your plan by objective: identify what you already use confidently, what you only understand in theory, and what you have never configured or troubleshot.

## Where Do I Start?

### Study Guide
:::tip
Replace `examcode` in `https://aka.ms/examcode-studyguide` with the lowercase exam code. For example: `https://aka.ms/az104-studyguide`.
:::

The study guide defines the skills measured and is the source of truth for the exam scope. Treat practical objectives as tasks you should be able to perform, explain, and troubleshoot rather than facts to recognize in a course.

## How to Prepare

Role-based exams are designed to validate job-role skills. Courses and practice tests can build and assess knowledge, but they do not replace experience making decisions and working with the product.

:::note
Microsoft Learn training and Microsoft product documentation serve different purposes. Training teaches topics in a structured sequence; documentation explains current settings, permissions, limits, commands, architecture, and troubleshooting. Use documentation during hands-on work whenever you need to understand what a setting does, which role or SKU is required, or how one configuration affects another.
:::

### Using MSFTHub Study Guide Courses

Rather than hunting down matching training modules and product documentation for every objective manually, check the **Text** tab on your exam's page for its **Study Guide Course**. Each course is organized around the official study guide task by task:

- Review the **Audience profile** and each skill group's **Fundamentals** block first if a service or concept is unfamiliar.
- Under each study-guide task, use the **Learning** tab to learn the topic and the **Documentation** tab for exact settings, SKUs, roles, limits, and troubleshooting while you practice in a lab environment.
- If the exam is in beta without an official Microsoft Learn course yet, or has an upcoming skills-measured update with dual current/upcoming tabs, the Study Guide Course keeps your reading aligned to the exact version you are sitting.

### Should I Take a Fundamentals Exam First?

If you are new to Microsoft products or cloud technologies, it might be beneficial to start with a fundamental exam. See [How to prepare for Microsoft Fundamentals Exams](/certs/prepare/fundamentals/) for the recommended preparation path.

Fundamentals exams provide a broad overview and foundational knowledge, which can make role-based material more approachable.

They can also introduce Microsoft's question style and the certification process. However, they do not replace the product depth or hands-on experience expected by a role-based exam.

### Preparation Workflow

<Steps>
1. Turn every study-guide objective into a tracker, assess your current confidence, and check the exam page's **Study Guide Changes** card for any upcoming or recent objective updates.
2. Work through the exam's **Study Guide Course** (in the **Text** tab of your exam page) task by task, reading skill-group **Fundamentals** and **Learning** modules first and **Documentation** pages alongside them, or pair the official Microsoft Learn course with product documentation (using [Navigating Microsoft Certification Study Materials](/certs/prepare/studymaterials/) and [Microsoft Exam Study Materials](/certs/) if you need an alternative primary course).
3. For each practical objective, perform the task in a suitable environment while consulting Microsoft product documentation. Record what changed, why you chose that configuration, and how you would verify or troubleshoot it.
4. Repeat important tasks without step-by-step instructions. If you do not have access through work, use an eligible trial, developer environment, sandbox, or training environment described in [Hands-on Exam Practice](/certs/prepare/labs/), then find exercises in [Microsoft Exams Labs](/certs/labs/) (or via **Exam Labs** on your exam page).
5. Take the official Practice Assessment if one is available. Research every incorrect or uncertain answer and return to the matching objective, documentation, and hands-on task.
6. Use fresh questions and scenario variations to confirm that you understand the decision, not just the wording of one question bank.
7. Practice the interface in the [Microsoft Certification Exam Sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo) and review the [Exam Experience](/certs/guide/takingtheexams/) guide before your appointment.
</Steps>

### Microsoft Learn During the Exam

:::caution
Currently, Microsoft Learn is not available during any GitHub exams.
:::

Microsoft currently provides Microsoft Learn access during role-based exams. See [Microsoft Learn during your exam](/certs/guide/takingtheexams/#microsoft-learn-during-your-exam) for guidance on using it effectively. The exam timer continues, external domains are blocked, and Practice Assessments, Q&A, and your profile are unavailable. Use Learn for targeted lookups rather than as a substitute for preparation.

## How to Know When You Are Ready

You should be able to perform applicable objectives without a walkthrough, explain the tradeoffs behind your choices, locate specific documentation efficiently, and troubleshoot common failures. Remaining mistakes should be isolated rather than concentrated in an entire objective domain.
